Method

What the bot actually does.

No part of this is a score projection. The whole system reads one thing — the gap between where the money is and where the tickets are — and bets that the number keeps moving.

The signal

Money against tickets

Sportsbooks report two different shares on a market: the percentage of bets on each side, and the percentage of money. When those two disagree sharply, a small number of large bets are on one side and a large number of small bets are on the other. The book screens for that gap, and for how far the price has already moved from its opening number. A pick needs both. A crowd it disagrees with but a line that has not moved is not a signal; a line that has moved for a reason everyone can already read — a starter ruled out, a goalie confirmed — is not one either.

This is not a value model, and it is worth being exact about that. Fair value here is derived from the same prices the book is betting into, so the entry is always roughly fair-minus-vig. There is no independent estimate of who should win. The only honest test of a system like this is whether the market agreed with it afterwards — which is why closing-line value, and not the win rate, is the headline number on every page of this site.

Prices and grading

Where the numbers come from

Entry prices are FanDuel on almost every pick. Two exceptions worth naming: a little over twenty football and hockey picks are priced on Polymarket, and the NBA board records whichever book had the best number at the time — which is part of why the NBA page is measured in points rather than percent. Closing prices are captured before the game starts, never looked up afterwards. A pick with no captured close is shown with a dash rather than an estimate.

CLV is reported in percentage points of implied probability: the implied probability of the closing price minus the implied probability of the entry price. A positive number means the market finished shorter than the price the pick was written at.

Honesty rules

Things this site will not do

Records are frozen once graded and never re-priced. Sports where CLV is negative stay on the page with the negative number visible. There is no crowned play of the day, because most of the entry gates behind these picks have not yet collected enough closes to be judged, and publishing a daily headline pick would imply a confidence the data does not support yet.

Picks have been removed from this log once, and it flattered the record. A long price does not meet the entry rule, so those picks are no longer logged at all. In October 2026 that rule was also applied backwards, which deleted 14 soccer picks that had gone 2-10 and moved the published record from −9 to −1. Mean CLV barely moved, 0.62% to 0.59%, which is the measure this book is actually judged on — but the win-loss figure improved because losing picks were taken out, and a page that did not say so would be hiding the one edit that made it look better.

Voided picks were counted as picks until 28 October 2026, and dropping them flattered the book. A row is voided when it should never have been logged in the first place. Almost all of these come from a single re-screen on 4 October that ran without a horizon and logged the following Saturday's games six days early, at soft early-week numbers the entry rule was never built for. Those 47 rows were withdrawn at the time — but the code that generates this site never read the field, so they stayed inside every pick count on every page. Eight of them had already had a closing price captured and three had been graded, which put them inside the published CLV and the published record as well. Taking them out moves the book from 734 picks to 687, lifts mean CLV from +0.263% to +0.306%, and removes two wins and one loss. The correction is right, because a withdrawn pick is not a pick — but it is also a change that makes the numbers look better, so it is named here instead of made quietly.
