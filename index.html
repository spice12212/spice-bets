Closing-line value by week

Each bar is the average CLV of every pick graded that week, in percentage points of implied probability. The number beneath it is how many closes it is built on; weeks under five are left out, because at that size the bar says more about the sample than the book.

How a pick gets here

Three steps, in order, every time.

1

Screen for disagreement

Every market on the board is read for the gap between where the money is and where the tickets are, and for how far the number has already travelled from its opener. A pick needs both: a crowd it disagrees with, and a line already moving.

2

Log it at the price offered

The pick is written down with the price at that moment and is never re-priced afterwards. The number shown here today is the number that was here when it was written.

3

Capture the close, then grade

A scheduled job takes the closing price before the game starts — not a remembered one, not one looked up later. The pick is then graded on the result and on whether the entry beat that close. Both go on the record, win or lose.

What this is not

The limits, stated up front rather than in the small print.

It is paper. These are screener outputs, logged and graded. No figure on this site represents money staked, and there is no bankroll behind it.

There is no headline play of the day. Most of the entry gates behind these picks are unproven or uncalibrated, so the book does not crown a daily pick in public. That stays off until the gates earn it.

The straight record is a coin flip, and that is expected. A movement system is not a projection model. If you want a score prediction for tonight's game, this is the wrong page.

Negative numbers stay up. Two of the boards are below zero on CLV and are shown that way. The record is the whole record, not the good months.
