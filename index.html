So the measure that matters here is not the win-loss record. It is closing-line value: did the price beat where the market finished? That number is published below for every sport, including the sports where it is negative.

The NBA row is measured in points, not percent, and the two do not average together. Those picks are spreads, where the number itself moves; the rest of the book is totals and moneylines, where only the price moves. A points figure is the honest unit for a spread. On the 12 NBA picks whose line did not move at all, the price comparison works and comes to +0.21%. Every NBA pick is also paper in a stricter sense than the rest of this page — see the NBA page.

Read the CLV column, not the record column. A screener that bets into the same prices it derives fair value from always enters at roughly fair-minus-vig. What it is really betting is that the number keeps moving. Only CLV can tell you whether that happened, and on this book it has happened in soccer and hockey and has not happened in baseball or the NFL.

Mean CLV by week

The trend, not the headline

Each bar is the average closing-line value of every pick graded that week, in percentage points of implied probability. The number under each bar is how many closes it is built on. Weeks with fewer than five are left out, because at that size the bar says more about the sample than about the book.

How a pick gets here

01

Screen the board for disagreement

Every market on the board is read for the gap between where the money is and where the tickets are, and for how far the number has already travelled from its opener. A pick needs both: a crowd it disagrees with, and a line already moving.

02

Log it at the price it was offered

The pick is written down with the FanDuel price at that moment and is never re-priced afterwards. The number here today is the number that was here when it was written.

03

Capture the close, then grade

A scheduled job takes the closing price before the game starts — not a remembered one, not one looked up later. The pick is then graded on the result and on whether the entry beat that close. Both go on the record, win or lose.

What this is not

The limits, stated up front

It is paper. These are screener outputs, logged and graded. They are not a record of money staked.

There is no daily headline play here. Four of the five entry gates are either unproven or uncalibrated, so the book does not publish a crowned pick of the day. That stays off until the gates earn it.

The straight record is a coin flip, and that is expected. A movement system is not a projection model. If you are looking for a score prediction for tonight's game, this is the wrong page.

Negative numbers stay on the page. MLB and the NFL are both below zero on CLV and are shown that way. The record is the whole record, not the good months.
