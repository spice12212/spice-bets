Spread boards are in points, not percent, and the two do not average. On a spread the number moves, and a price-only percentage scores that as nothing. On the picks whose line held, the price comparison still works: +0.28% in college football over 102, -0.02% in the NFL over 43, +0.09% in the NBA over 27.

Moving football to points raised the headline, so here is what it was. Until 6 November 2026 football was pooled into the site-wide percentage. Taking it out moves that figure from +0.358% over 722 closes to +0.53% over 418 — up, because it removed the two weakest boards. Both sit at essentially nothing in their own unit: -0.02 points a pick in college football, +0.05 in the NFL. Named here rather than made quietly.

Closing-line value by week

Each bar is the average CLV of every percent-scored pick graded that week, in percentage points of implied probability. The spread boards - college football, the NFL and the NBA - are measured in points and are not in these bars. The number beneath each one is how many closes it is built on; weeks under five are left out, because at that size the bar says more about the sample than the book.

The process

Four steps, in order, every time.

1

Read the line

where it opened

Every market on the board is read against its opening number, from the book's own price history rather than a remembered one. A line that has not moved is not a spot.

2

Find the move

and who moved it

The gap between where the money is and where the tickets are. A pick needs both halves: a crowd it disagrees with, and a number already travelling away from them.

3

Check the timeline

take the other side

When did the move land, and what had already happened by then? A three-point move that finished five days ago is settled history, not a live read — and the pick is logged at the price offered, never re-priced afterwards.

4

Touch grass

optional

Then a scheduled job takes the closing price before kickoff and grades the pick on the result and on whether the entry beat that close. Both go on the record, win or lose, whatever anyone was doing at the time.

Bot Play of the Day

One pick a day gets crowned. It is not posted here, and it has not been posted publicly since 21 October 2026.

What the crown actually is

Of every pick the screeners log on a given day, one is flagged as the best play. It is ranked purely on price action — where the money sits against where the tickets sit, and how far the number has travelled since it opened. That is the same measure every board on this site is graded by.

What it is not

It is not a confidence rating and it is not a read on who wins. Two things worth knowing before anyone treats it as one: a majority of crowns are decided by a divergence score that is largely structural rather than a read of the crowd, and the highest-scoring band has so far earned the worst closing-line value in the book while winning the most games. That is a small sample and it is being watched rather than acted on, but it is the honest state of it.

Why it came off X

Four of the five entry gates behind these picks are unproven — written before the data existed and frozen under pre-registration until they can be reviewed fairly. A daily public pick coming out of unproven gates reads as a tip service, which this is not. It goes to members, with the write-up, where it can be followed properly rather than screenshotted.

What this is not

The limits, stated up front rather than in the small print.

It is paper. These are screener outputs, logged and graded. No figure on this site represents money staked, and there is no bankroll behind it.

There is no headline play of the day. Most of the entry gates behind these picks are unproven or uncalibrated, so the book does not crown a daily pick in public. That stays off until the gates earn it.

The straight record is a coin flip, and that is expected. A movement system is not a projection model. If you want a score prediction for tonight's game, this is the wrong page.

Negative numbers stay up. Two of the boards are below zero on CLV and are shown that way. The record is the whole record, not the good months.
